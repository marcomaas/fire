      What this map shows

      The red area is the mapped burnt area as assessed from satellite imagery by the Copernicus Emergency Management Service. The sequence moves from one acquisition to the next, showing how the mapped area changed over the days. The overlaid outline places a city at the same location for comparison, preserving its true area so the order of magnitude becomes directly legible.

      This is a selection: five fires for which Copernicus has published a mapping with at least two acquisitions. More of Europe is burning than is shown here.

      How to read the figures

      Timestamp
      Acquisition time of the underlying satellite scene, not the time of publication. All times in Central European Summer Time.
      Timeline
      The axis is proportional to real time and starts at the reported outbreak (triangle), not at the first acquisition. The hatched section is the interval between outbreak and first satellite acquisition — nothing is mapped there, so the animation cannot show it. Depending on the fire that is several hours to days. Each vertical mark is one acquisition; their spacing shows how densely the fire was mapped. Playback speed follows the same spacing.
      Between two acquisitions
      Timestamp and area figure are carried forward linearly. A presentational aid, not a measurement: what happened between two overpasses cannot be known from this data.
      Area figure
      Sum of all mapped patches for that state. How change is shown depends on the fire: where one contiguous area dominates, its outline is morphed from state to state. Where the fire breaks into many separate patches, each state is faded in as a whole and stays — morphing would suggest standstill there while the total area grows. The second case currently applies to most of the fires.
      When the area shrinks
      This happens. Burnt area does not disappear again, but mapped area can: a later assessment delineates more precisely and excludes unburnt islands counted before. The figure may therefore fall while the red area on the map stays unchanged.
      City comparison
      The outline is moved to the fire location and keeps its area — not its shape. Because a degree of longitude is shorter at higher latitudes, the east-west extent is corrected; for distant cities that compresses the outline by more than a fifth. It works as a measure of area, not as a city street plan.
